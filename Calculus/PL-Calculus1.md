### $m = \frac{Y_2 - Y_1}{X_2 - X_1}$ - Calculus 1 Lecture 0.1: Lines, Angle of Inclination, and the Distance Formula

#### Introduction to Slope
- Discusses how lines do not curve, extend infinitely, and require a minimum of two points or one point and a slope to be graphed [00:00:37].
- The slope ($m$) measures how a line rises or falls, calculated using the change in $y$ over the change in $x$ [00:01:17].
- The general slope formula is derived by assigning coordinates $(X_1, Y_1)$ and $(X_2, Y_2)$ to two generic points [00:01:31]:
  $$m = \frac{Y_2 - Y_1}{X_2 - X_1}$$

#### Point-Slope Form
- By fixing one point $(X_1, Y_1)$ and letting the other point vary as $(X, Y)$, the slope formula is manipulated into the point-slope equation [00:06:06]:
  $$Y - Y_1 = m(X - X_1)$$

#### Slope-Intercept Form
- Distributing the point-slope form yields the slope-intercept equation [00:14:36]:
  $$Y = mX + B$$
- Here, $m$ represents the slope and $B$ represents the $y$-intercept [00:15:34].

#### Horizontal and Vertical Lines
- **Horizontal Lines:** Occur when $Y$ equals a constant ($Y = C$), resulting in a slope of zero [00:17:38].
- **Vertical Lines:** Occur when $X$ equals a constant ($X = C$), resulting in an undefined slope [00:18:41].

#### Parallel and Perpendicular Lines
- **Parallel Lines:** Share the exact same slope [00:23:05].
- **Perpendicular Lines:** Meet at a $90^\circ$ angle and have slopes that are negative reciprocals of each other [00:24:12].

#### Angle of Inclination
- The angle of inclination ($\Phi$) is the angle a line makes with the $x$-axis [00:32:29].
- Relates to slope using the tangent trigonometric function [00:33:43]:
  $$m = \tan(\Theta)$$
- Inverse tangent ($\tan^{-1}$) can be utilized to find the angle when the slope is known [00:39:50].

#### Distance Formula
- Derived using the Pythagorean theorem ($a^2 + b^2 = c^2$) applied to the horizontal and vertical differences between two points [00:46:17].
- Expressed as [00:47:48]:
  $$D = \sqrt{(X_2 - X_1)^2 + (Y_2 - Y_1)^2}$$
